Cerakote Laser Imaging Color Guide

Your 77 laser colors, each with a swatch sampled from its Cerakote.com product page, whether Cerakote recommends it for laser imaging, and the closest recommended color for the ones it doesn't.

Designing for laser imaging

Laser imaging shifts the tone of a single Cerakote color without removing it. Nothing is ablated and no base coat shows through, so the coating keeps protecting the part.

Start from the right color

Not every color images well. Cerakote publishes a recommended list, and 24 of your 77 colors are on it (marked Imaging pick). For any other color, each card names the closest recommended color in the same series, so you can steer a customer toward something that will image.

Design in tones, not colors

  • The art is tone-on-tone: one coating color, with each laser setting giving a different shade of it.
  • Build artwork in grayscale with a small, fixed set of tones, and put each tone on its own layer mapped to a tested laser setting.
  • Avoid gradients that rely on more tone steps than you've dialed in for that color.

Build a tone library per color

  • Cerakote recommends a 20–60 W fiber laser.
  • Run their "square test": a power-vs-frequency grid on 3"×6" coated panels, changing pulse width, line spacing or speed between runs. 500–800 kHz is their suggested starting range.
  • Short pulse widths, high frequencies and faster speeds generally work better.
  • Settings change with laser make, model and wattage, so save one panel per color code.

Caveats

Hex values are sampled from Cerakote's product photos (swatch panels for H-Series, the coated tumbler for F-Series), so they are close but lit, and metallic colors are the least accurate. "Closest recommended" is a color-distance match on those samples, so confirm it against physical swatches.
